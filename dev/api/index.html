API

MeromorphicLoci.AAA — Type
AAA(; nsamples = 320, tol = 1e-13, max_degree = 150, stagnation = 5)

Zeros and poles of f(z) from a rational fit to its samples (the AAA algorithm). zdomain is either a box (zlo, zhi) of opposite complex corners, sampled evenly at about nsamples points, with loci reported inside it; or an array of sample points, with loci reported wherever the fit finds them.

  • nsamples — the fit finds at most about half as many zeros and poles as it has samples; raise it for a box that holds many.
  • tol — the fit stops once it matches the samples to tol relative to their largest magnitude, so a locus whose effect on the samples is smaller goes unfound. Noise in f above tol stalls the fit short (fitconverged = false) with spurious and split candidates: the confirming windings drop the first and count the multiplicity of the second, but loci buried in the noise (multiple zeros first) stay lost.
  • max_degree, stagnation — the fit stops at this degree, or after this many steps without improving.

unresolved lists candidates f was too noisy or undefined around to confirm. Samples where f is NaN are skipped, so return NaN where it cannot be trusted. A zero and a pole much closer together than the samples are not reported.

MeromorphicLoci.ArgumentPrinciple — Type
ArgumentPrinciple(; zres, minsep=nothing, zoom=4)

Adaptive refinement of (z, p...) near the zero/pole loci by phase winding. zdomain = (zlo, zhi) gives opposite complex corners of the z-rectangle. pdomain gives one entry per parameter: an interval (lo, hi), sampled at 9 equispaced knots, or a sorted vector of knots.

  • zres — z-plane resolution of the reported loci, and the scale at which they are told apart: loci closer than about zres fuse into one branch of their net winding. Two simple zeros read as one double zero; a zero–pole pair reads 0 until zoom separates it.
  • minsep — cells are refined to this size before any is discarded. Distinct loci closer than minsep can cancel inside one cell and vanish silently, so keep it below their closest spacing. It also sets the cost floor, about 2·area/minsep² evaluations per parameter knot. nothing means the z-diagonal / 8.
  • zoom — halvings below zres a branch of indeterminate winding is refined to separate a fused zero–pole pair. Winding 0 past that means a pair closer than zres/2^zoom, or loci crossing at some p.

Loci are discovered at the parameter knots; one existing only between two knots can be missed. Between knots p is refined only as finely as the loci move, so evaluations concentrate where loci meet or a zero–pole pair annihilates.

What else it can miss or leave indeterminate:

  • a zero or pole of order ≥ 3 — occasionally at order 3, often from order 4; divide known factors out of f;
  • a locus within about a cell of the box edge, so pad the box;
  • loci touching another: a zero of f along all of p (a symmetry-forced factor z^k, say) fuses with every locus crossing it; divide such factors out of f first.

f is evaluated across all Julia threads: f(p...) and the functions it returns must be thread-safe. How the search works: docs/src/argument_principle.md.

MeromorphicLoci.muller — Method
muller(f, x0; h, radius = Inf, tol = 1e-10, maxiter = 100)
muller(f, x0, x1, x2; tol = 1e-10, maxiter = 100)

Muller's method for a zero of f, from a guess x0 uncertain by about h or from three starting points. Returns (; root, converged, nevals): root is the smallest-|f| point evaluated (NaN if none was finite), a zero when converged, i.e. a step fell below tol·max(|root|, h) (h the starting points' spread). Steps stay within radius of x0; keep h and radius below the distance to any other zero, or it may converge there.

Where noise or rounding in f limits accuracy above tol, as at a multiple zero, root lands about that limit away, typically unconverged.
