How ArgumentPrinciple searches

ArgumentPrinciple refines a tree of cells over (z, p...) toward the loci of zeros and poles, reading only the phase of f at cell corners. It then links the surviving cells into branches and labels each one by an argument-principle winding. This page describes those steps and where they can fail.

Lattice

The z-box is halved along both axes together, so every cell has the box's aspect, down to zres, and a few levels further for escalation. Each parameter interval between knots is halved dyadically.

Scan

The scan refines breadth-first. A cell coarser than minsep is split first. Below that, a cell fires when one of its z-faces (one per corner of its parameter extent) has a nonzero winding of the corner quadrants walked around it. A step of two quadrants is an ambiguous ±π turn, which also fires. A face enclosing one simple zero reads +4, one simple pole −4.

A locus moving fast in p can cross a cell between its z-faces, so no face fires. The grazing guard catches it: corners spanning at least 3 quadrants mean the sheets Re f = 0 and Im f = 0 both cross the cell, and they meet only on loci.

Firing cells split in z down to zres. A parameter axis splits only where a locus leaves the cell's z-column over the cell's parameter step and no neighbouring column picks it up. That is, the locus moves more than a cell per step, so adjacency could no longer link it. Parameter resolution therefore follows the loci's speed rather than zres, and samples crowd where |dz/dp| → ∞ (two loci meeting) or a zero–pole pair annihilates.

Linking and labels

Firing cells at zres are grouped into connected branches. Two cells are connected when their z-columns are equal or adjacent and their parameter ranges touch.

A branch is labelled by the winding of f around its outline: at a parameter slice, the branch's z-columns grown by one column. The outline holds every locus of the branch, so a fused opposite partner cancels. It holds no other branch's loci, since the cells of another branch are at least two columns away. The winding is computed at up to three slices where one of the branch's faces fires. If every usable vote has one sign, the median vote is the label.

Each outline edge is integrated by bisection until every half-step turns the phase by less than π/2 and changes |f| by less than 2×. The phase test alone would miss a half-step that turns by an extra full turn, which a multiple locus hugging the edge causes. Such a locus also swings |f| across the half-step, which the modulus test catches.

Corner faces are not trusted for labels. They only flag a branch that encloses faces of both signs, which may hold a zero and a pole fused together.

Escalation

A branch whose faces show both signs, or whose votes disagree in sign or are all unusable, is refined one z level below zres, regrouped, and labelled again. This repeats up to zoom levels. A zero–pole pair a few sub-cells apart splits into cleanly labelled branches. A pair that stays connected down to the last level keeps winding 0. An outline that would leave the box withholds its vote, so a locus within a column of the box edge also escalates, and stays unresolved if it is still that close at the last level.

Aliasing

The scan decides from corner quadrants alone whether a cell may hold something. It is blind in two ways.

  • Pairs. Two opposite loci in one cell cancel on its faces. A zero–pole pair's phase also fades within a few pair-widths, so nothing marks the cell. Refining every cell to minsep before any is discarded bounds this. A lone simple locus always fires a face or the grazing guard, at any minsep.
  • Multiplicity. A k-fold locus turns the phase k times around a face, and four corners read that in quadrant steps. A triple zero centred in a face turns 270° per edge and reads −4. A 4-fold one turns a whole number of turns per edge, so all corners share a quadrant and the face reads 0. (z − z0)^k looks the same at every scale, so refining does not help. Labels are not affected, because outlines are integrated adaptively; but a k-fold locus that no corner reveals is never discovered.
