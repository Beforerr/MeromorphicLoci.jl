How Continuation follows loci

Write $f(z; p)$ for f(p)(z), $f' = \partial_z f$, $B$ for the box in $z$, and $p_1 < \dots < p_K$ for the knots. Continuation(seed) finds the zeros and poles of $f(\cdot\,; p_k)$ in $B$ with a point finder, the seeder, and follows each from knot to knot.

Following one locus

A simple zero $z^*(p)$ continues analytically while $f'(z^*; p) \ne 0$: until it meets another zero or a pole, or leaves $B$. A pole is followed as a zero of $1/f$, and a locus of constant multiplicity $m \ge 2$ as a simple zero of $f/f'$.

Each step extrapolates the last samples (degree $\le 2$ in $p$) to the next $p$ and corrects with Muller's method. A track ends at the next knot, on $\partial B$, or stuck: no step is accepted however short.

What is certified

A Branch asserts that its samples lie on one locus. Write $z_j$ for the other zeros and poles of $f$ in $B$, of winding $w_j$. Near a simple zero $z^*$,

\[f(z) = (z - z^*)\,q(z), \qquad d = f'(z^*) = q(z^*) = c \prod_j (z^* - z_j)^{w_j},\]

with $c$ free of zeros and poles in $B$, varying slowly. A track follows the germ $(z^*, d)$: when $z^*$ and a simple $z_j$ exchange places, $z^* - z_j$ changes sign and $\arg d$ turns by $\pi$. A step to $p$ is accepted if

\[|z^*(p) - \hat z(p)| \le \tau, \qquad |\log d(p) - \log \hat d(p)| \le \tfrac12,\]

with $\hat z$, $\hat d$ extrapolated from earlier samples ($\log \hat d$ linear in $p$) and $\tau$ set by zres. As another locus approaches, $\log d$ turns faster and steps shorten, and $\tau$ shrinks against the distance to the nearest other locus,

\[R = \left|\frac{2f'}{f''}\right|_{z^*} = \left|\frac{q}{q'}\right|_{z^*}.\]

Below the floor $\delta$, zfloor, nothing is told apart. Across a stretch with $R < \delta$, the turn $\Delta = \arg(d_\text{out}/d_\text{in})$ tells what happened:

  • \[\Delta \approx 0\]

    or $\pi$: the loci passed or crossed. The track is followed through, and the branch lists the stretch in uncertified.

  • \[\Delta \approx \pm\pi/2\]

    : two loci of a kind met at a branch point, $z_\pm \approx a \pm b\sqrt{p - p_0}$, and which outgoing locus continues which incoming one has no answer. The track ends there, uncertified. Expected where real roots of a real $f$ collide and leave as a complex pair.

At each knot, arriving tracks are matched to the seeds. A locus found at any knot is followed through the knots where the seeder missed it, and one that entered $B$ between knots is followed back from where it was found.

These checks rest on estimates, not theorems: $d$ and $R$ are leading-order readings, and an exchange among three loci equally close could leave $d$ unturned. The step control follows a priori path tracking for polynomial homotopies [TVV]; $1/R$ is the leading term of Smale's $\gamma$ in certified tracking [BL].

The count

Tracks keep to their own loci beside an unseeded one, but do not report it. What notices a seeder that came up short is the argument principle at each knot,

\[N(p_k) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \oint_{\partial B} \frac{f'}{f}\, dz = \#\text{zeros} - \#\text{poles},\]

counted with multiplicity, against $\sum_b w_b$ over the branches at $p_k$. The Survey's deficit lists the knots where they differ, and those where $N$ cannot be read: a locus on $\partial B$, or $f$ unusable along it. $N$ cannot see a zero and a pole missing together, nor anything between knots. An ArgumentPrinciple seeder computes $N$ anyway; for others it costs one sampling of $\partial B$ per knot, skipped with certify = false.

Several parameters

The knots span a grid $K_1 \times \dots \times K_M$. Every node is seeded, and every grid line, one $p_i$ varying with the others at knots, is followed as above. A locus found at any node is followed along every line through the nodes it reaches.

A branch is a set of node loci connected by tracks along any lines. Two zeros generically coincide only at isolated points of a parameter plane, and continuation around such a point swaps them: tracks around a grid cell holding one connect two loci of one node, and the branch has degree 2. A branch of degree 1 is a single-valued $z^*(p)$.

For real $f$ of real parameters, meetings lie along whole curves, where real roots collide and leave as a complex pair: the branches on either side end on the curve, uncertified at each grid line that crosses it.

What it can miss

  • A locus in $B$ at no knot: one living only within $(p_k, p_{k+1})$, or entering after $p_{K-1}$ and gone by $p_K$.
  • A locus the seeder misses at every knot it is in $B$ at. One found at any knot is followed to all the others it reaches.
  • Loci of noisy $f$: the corrector needs $f$ accurate to about $10^{-10}$ relative, and to $10^{-10m}$ at a locus of multiplicity $m$. Tracks then end stuck: keep known multiple factors factored. At a branch cut tracks end stuck too.

  • \[f\]

    that jumps in $p$, in scale or phase (a piecewise definition, a normalisation that switches): $d$ does not continue across the jump, and tracks end stuck there. A factor that varies rapidly in $p$ only costs steps: divide it out.
  • A knot placed exactly where loci meet cuts their branches there, uncertified. Move the knot.
  • Over several parameters, what only the inside of a grid cell shows: loci in $B$ only there, loci connected only there (separate branches), and two swap points in one cell, whose swaps undo each other (two branches of degree 1). A finer grid resolves each.

Cost

Seeding usually dominates, so the knots are the main cost. They serve only to discover loci: where none enters $B$ between them, $\{p_\text{lo}, p_\text{hi}\}$ is enough. Close approaches between loci cost the most steps. Accuracy does not depend on zres, and cost only weakly.

f(p) is called once per knot and once per step attempted, so setup work inside f(p) is paid per step and per locus.
