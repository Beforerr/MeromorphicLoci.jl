How Continuation follows loci

Continuation(seed) finds the zeros and poles of f(p) at a few parameter knots with a point finder, the seeder, and follows each of them from knot to knot. f is evaluated only at the knots and along the loci, inside the box, and every sample is a polished root. This page takes one parameter until Several parameters.

Following one locus

A simple zero z*(p) continues analytically in p for as long as it stays simple: until it meets another zero or a pole, or leaves the box. A pole is followed as a zero of 1/f, and a locus of constant multiplicity m ≥ 2 as a zero of f/f′, which is simple there.

Each step extrapolates the track's last samples to the next p and corrects the prediction with Muller's method. A track ends at the next knot, at the box edge, or stuck, where no step is accepted however short.

What is certified

A Branch asserts that its samples are points of one locus. To hold that, a track follows the germ of its locus: the root z* and the derivative d = f′(z*) there. d is the product of the separations of z* from every other zero and pole of f, seeded or not, times a slowly varying factor, so an exchange of two loci turns it by a half turn. A step is accepted if both the root and log d land on their predictions, and steps shorten against whatever locus closes in. Both d and the distance R = |2f′/f″| to the nearest other locus come from the corrector's own evaluations.

A multiple locus is followed on its root alone: f/f′ has derivative 1/m there whatever lies near, and the loci it could come apart into lie within the floor of it.

Closer than the floor zres/2^zoom nothing is told apart. Past such a stretch, the turn of d across it tells what happened:

  • a half turn or none: the loci passed or crossed. The track is followed through, and the branch lists the stretch in uncertified;
  • a quarter turn: two loci of a kind met and turned (a square-root branch point in p), and which outgoing locus continues which incoming one has no answer. The track ends there, uncertified. Expected where real roots of a real f collide and leave as a complex pair.

Each branch lists its own stretches, so two branches at one meeting list overlapping ones.

At the knots seeds are found independently of the tracks, and each arrival is matched to a seed of its kind:

  • an arrival on no seed is a locus the seeder missed: it becomes a seed and is followed on;
  • a seed no arrival reached is followed backward: it entered the box between the knots, or the seeder missed it at earlier knots, where it becomes a seed in turn;
  • two tracks arriving on one seed met there, and are uncertified.

A seed of winding |w| ≥ 2 is one multiple locus or simple ones closer than the seeder resolves; it is split into the loci it stands for before anything is followed. A seed that cannot be followed (kind unreadable, or polish failed) is a branch of that one sample, uncertified.

These checks rest on estimates, not theorems: d and R are leading-order readings, and an exchange among three loci equally close could leave d unturned. The step control follows a priori path tracking for polynomial homotopies [TVV]; 1/R is the leading term of Smale's γ in certified tracking [BL].

The count

Tracks keep to their own loci beside an unseeded one, but do not report it. What notices a seeder that came up short is a count at each knot: the winding of f around the box outline, zeros minus poles, against the branches there. Survey.deficit lists the knots where they differ as p => n, the box holding n more, and p => nothing where the outline cannot be read: a locus on the box edge, or f unusable along it. An ArgumentPrinciple seeder's own count is used; otherwise the outline is integrated as ArgumentPrinciple's certificate does it. The count cannot see a zero and a pole missing together, nor anything between knots.

Several parameters

The knots span a grid. Every node is seeded, and every grid line, one parameter varying between the knots of the others, is followed as above. A locus a track carries into a node where the seeder missed it becomes a locus of that node, and the other lines through the node are followed again from it.

A branch is a set of node loci connected by tracks along any lines. Two zeros generically coincide only at isolated points of a parameter plane, and continuation around such a point swaps them: tracks around a grid cell holding one connect two loci of one node, and the branch has degree 2. A branch of degree 1 is a single-valued z*(p).

Real f of real parameters has meetings along whole curves, where real roots collide and leave as a complex pair: the branches on either side end on the curve, uncertified at each grid line that crosses it.

What it can miss

  • A locus in the box at no knot: one living only between two knots, or entering after the second-to-last knot and gone by the last.
  • A locus the seeder misses at every knot it is in the box at. One found at any knot is followed to all the others it reaches.
  • Loci of noisy f: the corrector needs f accurate to about 10⁻¹⁰ relative, and a locus of multiplicity m to the power m. Tracks then end stuck: keep known multiple factors factored. At a branch cut tracks end stuck too.
  • f that jumps in p, in scale or phase (a piecewise definition, a normalisation that switches): d does not continue across the jump, and tracks end stuck there. A factor that varies rapidly in p only costs steps: divide it out.
  • A knot placed exactly where loci meet cuts their branches there, uncertified. Move the knot.
  • Over several parameters, what only the inside of a grid cell shows: loci in the box only there, loci connected only there (separate branches), and two swap points in one cell, whose swaps undo each other (two branches of degree 1). A finer grid resolves each.

Cost

Seeding usually dominates, so the knots are the main cost. They serve only to discover loci: where none enters the box between them, the two ends of the parameter range are enough. Following costs about 5–10 evaluations of f per step (three times that on f/f′), and a few hundred per locus for each close approach. Accuracy does not depend on zres, and cost only weakly.

f(p) is called once per knot and once per step attempted, so setup work inside f(p) is paid per step and per locus.
